About

I’m a master’s student in AI for Sustainable Development at UCL. Before that I read electronic engineering at the University of Southampton, where I mostly built hardware — sensors, meters, a small chip — and picked up two utility-model patents along the way.

My dissertation starts from a simple observation: eco-driving feedback is designed once, the same for every driver. What happens if an optimiser tunes it for each person instead? To find out, I built a VR driving simulator from real map data, ran a study with sixteen drivers, and let a multi-objective Bayesian optimiser search the interface design space with the driver in the loop.

I’m applying to PhD programmes in China for autumn 2027, around reinforcement learning, simulation, and human-in-the-loop learning.

Sustainable Personalised Driving

MSc dissertation, UCL. A VR driving simulator built on real map data; a multi-objective Bayesian optimiser personalises eco-driving HUD configurations for each of sixteen drivers over twenty-one rounds. Code is public.

Badminton stroke wearable

Undergraduate dissertation, University of Southampton. A wrist-worn device that classifies four stroke types with an SVM (88.95% accuracy) and turns the result into coach-style feedback.
